## Item ratings

### EV001 — Overall dimensions

**Direction:** `confirmed`  **Confidence:** B

**Reason:** The variable asks whether the overall bounding dimensions of the
corpus are established. The statement gives an explicit corpus-wide range with
the knob/no-knob distinction handled separately, which is the form a properly
characterised dimensional variable takes. It reads as a synthesis over a
catalogue rather than a single primary measurement, hence B rather than A. The
range is wide (2.5:1), but width of range is a fact about the corpus, not a
defect in the evidence for this variable.

### EV003 — Wall thickness

**Direction:** `confirmed`  **Confidence:** B

**Reason:** A corpus-wide range is given, so the property is characterised as a
rule. Two reservations, neither of which changes the direction. First, 0.5–4 mm
is an 8:1 spread, so "wall thickness" is not a single corpus value but a broad
band. Second, "remarkably thin-walled" is the source's qualitative judgement,
not a measurement, and does not add evidential weight beyond the range. B: an
authoritative synthesis, single unnamed source, no indication that the
extremes were re-measured.

### EV004 — Hole diameter distribution

**Direction:** `confirmed`  **Confidence:** B

**Reason:** This is the strongest-formed statement in the set. It gives not
only the overall range but the *within-specimen* distribution of extremes
(largest hole 1.7–4 cm, smallest 0.6–2.8 cm), which is what a distribution
variable actually requires. The clause "every sufficiently complete specimen
shows holes of differing size" is stated as a universal rule with an explicit
completeness qualifier, which is the honest way to state it. B: systematic
catalogue statistics, not a primary excavation report.

### EV005 — Opposite-hole relationships

**Direction:** `weak_absent`  **Confidence:** B

**Reason:** The variable asks about pairing and correspondence between opposite
holes. The statement is internally split and settles against a rule: Avenches
has *no* equal pair and two pairs differing by more than 9 mm; Vienne has two
exactly equal pairs but, on the assessment of the author who devised the
recording method, no other regularity; Jublains has four near-equal pairs and
two off by 4 and 6.5 mm. So correspondence holds in a real minority of measured
pairs and fails in the rest — precisely `weak_absent` rather than `ambiguous`,
because the statement does decide, and it decides against a general rule. The
"statistically between c 0.2 and c 0.45 cm" clause is not a counter: a typical
pair difference of 2–4.5 mm on holes of 6–40 mm is a large relative mismatch,
not evidence of correspondence. B: named specimens with recorded measurements.

### EV006 — Hole profile

**Direction:** `ambiguous`  **Confidence:** B

**Reason:** The definition asks whether holes are **cylindrical, tapered or
irregular** — a question about the bore through the wall thickness. The
statement answers a different question: how *round in plan* the openings are.
"Ten perfectly round holes" constrains the outline, not the axial profile, and
nothing in the statement distinguishes a parallel bore from a conical one. I
can also see no coverage beyond the commonest type. Evidence exists and is
relevant to "irregular", but it does not decide the variable as defined, so
`ambiguous` is the honest rating. B for the underlying observation (a named
published typology); the shortfall here is one of scope, not of source quality.

### EV008 — Knob diameter

**Direction:** `confirmed`  **Confidence:** B

**Reason:** Within-object uniformity is stated without qualification ("all of
the same size"), which is a corpus-wide rule. The between-object range does not
weaken it: the variable is knob diameter, and the statement characterises both
its within-object behaviour (constant) and its between-object behaviour (broad).
B: unqualified catalogue-level generalisation; no counts, tolerances or
measurement method are given, so I cannot grade it higher. Note that an
unqualified "all" in a corpus of this size is itself slightly suspicious — see
the tension flagged at EV009/EV038.

### EV009 — Knob symmetry

**Direction:** `confirmed`  **Confidence:** B

**Reason:** Regularity of knob placement is asserted as a rule (twenty knobs,
one per vertex) with a single named exception (Guggenberger no 66, three knobs
per vertex) and a stated universal floor (no specimen without knobs). A rule
with one named, countable exception is still a rule; the fact that the
exception is identified by catalogue number is a mark of a careful source
rather than a weakness. "Never pointed or intentionally faceted" is an
additional unqualified negative that I would grade lower on its own, but it is
not what the variable asks about. B.

### EV010 — Face symmetry

**Direction:** `confirmed`  **Confidence:** B

**Reason:** The variable names *deviation* from regular geometry, so the
property is the deviation itself, and the statement evidences it with numbers:
48–52 mm face-to-face depending on axis (an 8 per cent variation) on the only
stratified specimen, two oval openings at 21x26 mm, and five of twelve holes
with two differing diameters plus a probable ellipse on the best-measured
specimen. The reservation is sampling: a corpus-wide claim rests on two
specimens. It carries anyway, because those two are the *most* carefully
measured, so the direction of the sampling bias runs toward under-detecting
deviation elsewhere, not over-detecting it here. B rather than A: the values
are quoted at one remove and the generalising sentence is the summariser's, not
the excavator's.

### EV011 — Alloy composition

**Direction:** `confirmed`  **Confidence:** B

**Reason:** The corpus-level claim (copper alloy) is a rule, and one quantified
analysis is attached (XRF: Cu 75 / Sn 7 / Pb 18). B not A: XRF is a surface
technique, the figures are quoted without uncertainties or a stated number of
spots, only two specimens are cited for a corpus-wide claim, and the second
("bronze and lead") is a museum record rather than an analysis. Enough to fix
the material class as a rule; not enough to characterise the alloy corpus-wide,
which the definition arguably also asks for.

### EV012 — Casting quality

**Direction:** `weak_absent`  **Confidence:** C

**Reason:** Read as "is skilled, consistent casting quality a corpus-wide
property", the statement decides against it: some specimens were difficult to
cast, many British examples are judged less skilful and possibly imitations,
and PAS specimens consistently show a rough unfinished interior. But the poor
quality is attributed to identifiable subsets rather than to the corpus, and
skilled work is implied for the remainder — a general rule that fails while
holding in some cases, i.e. `weak_absent`. C rather than B: "do not appear to
be so skilfully designed" is an impressionistic aesthetic judgement with no
stated criterion, "many" and "some" are unquantified, and the imitation claim
is explicitly hedged ("may be"). The PAS observation is the firmest part and is
a finds-database record.

### EV013 — Casting defects

**Direction:** `weak_confirmed`  **Confidence:** B

**Reason:** Two reasons for not rating this `confirmed`. First, 70 per cent is
a majority but not a rule, and the variable asks about a corpus property.
Second and more important, what the statement actually evidences is not what
the definition names: *Produktionslöcher* and the less-round oversized opposed
pair are traces of the intended production sequence — the statement itself says
they are "explained by the production process" — not voids, seams or miscasts.
No porosity, no flash line, no failed cast is reported anywhere in the
statement. So the property as defined is evidenced only obliquely and only in
most-but-not-all cases. B: the 70 per cent figure implies a systematic count
over a catalogue.

### EV014 — Surface finishing

**Direction:** `ambiguous`  **Confidence:** B

**Reason:** The definition names **grinding, polishing, scraping** — abrasive
surface treatment. The statement is entirely about engraved concentric circles:
their coverage (ten of twelve faces on type 1a, all twelve on type 2a Mainz 3),
their count (two to six, three typical) and their inconsistency between
specimens. That is decoration, and its post-cast execution is the subject of
EV016. Nothing here reports whether any surface was ground, polished or
scraped. The statement is well-evidenced — a direct quotation from the
stratified specimen, corroboration on two further named specimens — but
well-evidenced about a different variable, so it cannot decide this one.
`ambiguous`, B. This is the item where I would most expect the recorded rating
to differ from mine, and the disagreement would be about variable scope rather
than about the evidence.

### EV016 — Tool marks

**Direction:** `confirmed`  **Confidence:** C

**Reason:** Engraving after casting is a secondary machining trace, and per the
coverage described at EV014 it is present across the common types, so as a
corpus rule the property holds. C rather than B because of what the statement
does *not* say: it reports a conclusion about technique, not an observation of
the traces themselves. No burin or graver marks are described, no chatter,
no direction of cut, no magnification, no metallography. "Engraved into the
metal after casting" is a technological inference — a sound and conventional
one — and it is graded here as an inference.

### EV017 — Internal hole wear

**Direction:** `weak_absent`  **Confidence:** C

**Reason:** Absence of wear is stated as the general condition, with "a few
exceptions" explicitly reserved — so the property fails as a rule but holds in
some cases, which is `weak_absent` rather than `absent`. C: the statement is
about the inner and outer surfaces at large, not specifically about the bores
of the openings, so it reaches this variable only by inclusion; and "do not
look worn" is macroscopic appearance, with no use-wear study, no microscopy and
no protocol cited. The carve-out for "later destruction and corrosion" also
cuts both ways — corrosion that obscures wear would produce exactly this
report.

### EV018 — External wear

**Direction:** `weak_absent`  **Confidence:** C

**Reason:** Same evidential act as EV017 but squarely on the variable, since
external surfaces are what the statement is primarily about. Rule of
non-wear with a reserved minority of exceptions → `weak_absent`. C for the same
reason: this is a naked-eye impression generalised over a corpus, not a
systematic wear analysis, and post-depositional corrosion is conceded to affect
the surfaces being judged.

### EV019 — Rope wear

**Direction:** `absent`  **Confidence:** D

**Reason:** Direction and confidence part company here, and deliberately. The
statement contains an observational hook — the general finding of unworn
surfaces is said to cover the holes and knobs, which are the very surfaces rope
would mark — so it does bear on the variable and it bears against the property:
`absent`. But its primary support is "no rope wear is reported anywhere", which
is an argument from the silence of the literature. Nobody in this statement
looked for rope wear and failed to find it; the reports simply do not mention
it, and a diagnostic that is not sought is not absent. The extension of a
generic non-wear observation to a specific wear signature is the summariser's
step, not a source's. D. **This is the item the prompt's own warning is
about**, and a rater who chose `ambiguous` here would have the better of the
methodological argument; I record `absent` only because of the holes-and-knobs
clause.

### EV020 — Rotational wear

**Direction:** `ambiguous`  **Confidence:** D

**Reason:** I rate this differently from EV019 on purpose. Here the statement
is nothing but the silence: "no evidence of repeated rotation is reported
anywhere". There is no accompanying positive observation of any surface, no
statement that the relevant surfaces were examined, and no indication that
rotational wear was ever a thing anyone looked for. A pure non-report does not
decide the variable in either direction, so `ambiguous` is the honest answer
rather than `absent`. D: the statement reveals no evidential basis at all
beyond the literature not mentioning it.

### EV031 — Military association

**Direction:** `weak_confirmed`  **Confidence:** B

**Reason:** Just under a fifth is a minority, and the statement says so
explicitly in the same breath ("the overwhelming majority derive from civilian
contexts"). The prompt's own worked example applies almost verbatim. The
frontier concentration is a real but secondary signal and does not lift a fifth
into a rule. B: proportions computed over located finds, which is
catalogue-grade work. Worth flagging that the denominator is finds *with a
recorded location*, a subset that is likely biased toward excavated (and hence
disproportionately military) sites, and that a findspot inside a camp is a
contextual association rather than the "direct military evidence" the
definition names — no specimen is reported associated with military equipment.

### EV032 — Ritual association

**Direction:** `weak_confirmed`  **Confidence:** B

**Reason:** 8.5 per cent sacred, 7 per cent funerary, 4 per cent riverine —
around a fifth even if all three are pooled, and pooling them is itself an
interpretive choice. A minority, therefore `weak_confirmed`. The named cases (a
cult precinct, the temple of Nodens at Lydney, a temple-attributed cache, three
richly furnished graves) are what raise this above a bare percentage: they are
individually identifiable and checkable. B. The river category is the weakest
leg — deposition in water is read as votive by convention, but loss and
riverbank erosion produce the same distribution.

### EV033 — Rod compatibility

**Direction:** `weak_confirmed`  **Confidence:** C

**Reason:** The definition asks whether rods fit **securely**. That holes of
9.4–40.6 mm admit a cylindrical rod is nearly a tautology and establishes fit,
not security: no seating, retention, friction or stop is described, and per
EV005 the opposite hole on the same axis frequently differs by millimetres, so
a straight rod that seats in one hole will often not seat in its opposite. The
Gelduba bone object is the real content here — c 15 cm by c 3 cm, immediately
adjacent to a specimen whose production holes are 24 and 23 mm — but adjacency
in the ground is a spatial association, not a demonstrated pairing, and a 30 mm
rod through a 24 mm hole does not pass. So the property is evidenced partially:
`weak_confirmed`. C: the geometric part is the project's own ASSESSMENT, not a
source; the archaeological part is an excavated association, which pulls the
grade up from D.

### EV034 — Rope compatibility

**Direction:** `confirmed`  **Confidence:** D

**Reason:** Direction and confidence again diverge. Routing cord through
openings of 6–40 mm and passing it over twenty proud vertex knobs is
geometrically unobstructed on any specimen in the corpus, so as a statement of
possibility the property holds as a rule: `confirmed`. But the statement is a
bare ASSESSMENT with no numbers whatever — no hole diameter, no knob diameter,
no cord gauge, no reference to any specimen, no experiment or replica. It
asserts its conclusion and cites nothing. D. That a claim is almost certainly
true does not make its stated basis anything better than an unsupported
assertion, and the low bar of "can it be done" is doing most of the work.

### EV035 — Structural stability

**Direction:** `ambiguous`  **Confidence:** D

**Reason:** Two claims, of very different quality, welded together. That the
form rests stably on any face is essentially geometric and true. That "a 3 mm
bronze wall resists modest compressive load" is unsupported and, worse, uses an
unrepresentative number: EV003 puts corpus wall thickness at 0.5–4 mm and calls
the objects remarkably thin-walled, so 3 mm sits near the top of the range and
is not the corpus case. "Modest" is undefined, no load is quantified, no
calculation or test is offered, and the objects are pierced by twelve holes,
which is exactly the geometry that makes a thin shell fail under load. Resting
stability and load-bearing stability are not the same property; the statement
establishes the first and asserts the second, so it does not decide the
variable: `ambiguous`. D: self-generated assessment, no calculation, favourable
choice of input value.

### EV036 — Load transfer

**Direction:** `confirmed`  **Confidence:** D

**Reason:** The variable asks only for *plausible* force paths, which is a low
bar, and six pairs of opposing faces is a certainty of the solid rather than a
claim about the artifacts — so the paths exist and the property holds:
`confirmed`. D for basis: this is the project's own ASSESSMENT restating
elementary geometry; no specimen is cited, no load is quantified, and no rod is
attested in place. The mismatch between opposite holes recorded at EV005 (up to
9 mm on Avenches, no equal pair at all) means several of those six nominal axes
would not take a single straight rod bearing on both faces, which the statement
does not address.

### EV037 — Assembly potential

**Direction:** `absent`  **Confidence:** C

**Reason:** The premise — a modular structural system needs interchangeable
components of consistent dimension — is sound, and the corpus fails it by a
wide margin: a 2.5:1 spread in overall diameter, on top of the hole-diameter
and pairing irregularity already recorded. Nothing in the corpus is
interchangeable with anything else, so the property is not present: `absent`.
C rather than B: the dimensional range is well-attested catalogue data, but the
modularity premise is the project's own reasoning, and strictly the argument
shows only that the corpus *as a whole* is not a component set. It does not
exclude a matched local pair or set — though no such set is reported, and
specimens are typically singleton finds.

### EV038 — Orientation dependence

**Direction:** `absent`  **Confidence:** B

**Reason:** No distinguished base, no axis, knobs at every vertex, equal
resting on every face: the property "requires fixed orientation" is not present.
The Arloff icosahedron is the strongest element of the statement — it shows
that a maker in the same tradition who *wanted* to mark an axis did so visibly,
by enlarging three knobs around one hole, which converts an absence into a
meaningful absence. B. One deduction from what would otherwise be a higher
grade: "every specimen carries knobs at all twenty vertices" is flatly stronger
than EV009's "almost all specimens carry twenty knobs, one at each vertex",
and the same source set cannot support both. The universal here is overstated,
which does not change the direction but does cap the confidence.

### EV039 — Standardisation

**Direction:** `absent`  **Confidence:** B

**Reason:** Every axis on which standardisation could show up is reported
varying: hole diameters 0.6–4 cm, overall diameter 4–10 cm, knob diameter broad,
decoration inconsistent, and — the decisive one, because it removes the escape
route that the *objects* differ while each object is internally regular — a
within-specimen coefficient of variation of hole diameter reaching 40 per cent.
The property is not present: `absent`. B: the ranges are catalogue-grade; the
CV and the arithmetic-progression test read as derived by the summariser rather
than quoted from a source, and no n, no per-specimen values and no method are
given for either, so I cannot check them.

### EV040 — Regional variation

**Direction:** `weak_confirmed`  **Confidence:** C

**Reason:** A genuine geographic pattern is claimed — a British group with
special features and lower execution quality — so the property is present, but
only as a minority tendency stated in hedged terms: "many", "some of which",
"may be local imitations". The remaining support is weaker than it looks. Several
typological groups being distinguished is not by itself a geographic fact
unless the groups map to regions, which the statement does not say, and
"inconsistent face decoration" is corpus-wide inconsistency (EV014, EV039)
rather than regional patterning. `weak_confirmed`, C: unquantified,
impressionistic, explicitly hedged, and partly resting on evidence that is not
geographic.

### EV044 — Interior finish and marking

**Direction:** `confirmed`  **Confidence:** C

**Reason:** The definition is compound — is the interior smoothed or as cast,
and does it carry marking — and the statement answers both the same way: as
cast, unmarked. The first half is well made, with six specimens quoted
individually and in agreement ("more roughly cast", "crudely cast",
"roughcast", "crudely finished"), which is a real corpus rule rather than a
generalisation from one case. The second half is the weak leg and sets the
grade: "no specimen in the database is recorded with interior engraving, scale,
marking or a smoothed working surface" is an absence in the *records*, not a
finding from inspection. Catalogue entries routinely omit interiors altogether
— the six quotations above are notable precisely because interiors are so
seldom described — so non-recording is close to uninformative here. Direction
`confirmed` on the strength of the six direct descriptions; C because the
negative half of the claim rests on silence.

**Which items were hardest to rate, and why?**

1. **EV014 (surface finishing)** — hardest by some distance. The statement is
   detailed, well-sourced and about something else. Deciding between
   `ambiguous` (the statement does not address grinding/polishing/scraping) and
   `confirmed` (engraving *is* a surface treatment, applied to most faces of
   most specimens) is a decision about how literally to read the definition,
   not about the evidence. I took the definition literally because EV016 exists
   and covers post-cast working, which implies the two variables were meant to
   divide that ground.
2. **EV019 vs EV020 (rope wear, rotational wear)** — the two statements are
   near-identical in form, both arguments from the silence of the literature,
   and I split them (`absent`/D vs `ambiguous`/D) on the single clause in EV019
   that extends a positive non-wear observation to the holes and knobs. That
   clause is thin to be carrying a direction change, and I could be argued out
   of it.
3. **EV013 (casting defects)** — because the statement's content
   (*Produktionslöcher*, an oversized opposed pair) is a description of the
   intended production sequence, not of defects. Rating "defects present at 70
   per cent" would import a reading the statement does not support; rating
   `absent` would ignore that the features are real casting-process traces.
4. **EV033/EV035 (rod compatibility, structural stability)** — both bundle a
   near-tautological geometric truth with an unsupported engineering claim, and
   the rating turns on which half the definition is really asking about
   ("securely", "under load").
5. **EV005 (opposite-hole relationships)** — genuinely mixed data, and the
   choice between `ambiguous` and `weak_absent` is close. I chose the latter
   because the statement does decide: no measured specimen shows a regular
   opposite-hole scheme.

The confidence grades are likely to be a larger source of disagreement than the
directions. I used no A grades at all, on the view that a statement summarised
into this document, without excavation context, uncertainties or method, cannot
present itself as a stratified peer-reviewed primary report even when the
underlying specimen is one; EV010 and EV011 are the candidates a more generous
rater would grade A.

**Did any statement strike you as resting on weaker evidence than its wording
suggests?** Yes — six, in rough order of severity.

1. **EV020, then EV019.** "No evidence of X is reported anywhere in the corpus"
   is a statement about the literature, not about the objects. Neither
   statement says anyone examined a surface looking for the feature and failed
   to find it. Wear signatures that nobody searches for are reliably not
   reported. EV019 at least gestures at a positive observation; EV020 does not,
   and its confident phrasing ("no evidence... anywhere") reads as a finding
   when it is a silence.
2. **The four ASSESSMENT items — EV033, EV034, EV035, EV036.** These are not
   sourced statements at all; the "ASSESSMENT:" prefix marks them as the
   project's own reasoning. Three of the four cite no specimen. EV034 cites no
   number of any kind. They read in the same voice and the same format as the
   sourced items, and in a table of directions and confidences they would be
   indistinguishable from them. EV035 additionally selects 3 mm as its wall
   thickness when the corpus range is 0.5–4 mm and the same corpus is elsewhere
   called remarkably thin-walled — a favourable input choice that is not
   flagged.
3. **EV035's "resists modest compressive load"** deserves separate mention. No
   load is stated, no calculation is shown, no test is described, and the
   qualifier "modest" is unfalsifiable. It is the only quasi-engineering claim
   in the set and it has the least support of anything here.
4. **EV044's second half.** "No specimen in the database is recorded with
   interior engraving, scale or marking" is an absence in the records
   masquerading as an absence in the objects. Interiors are seldom described at
   all — which is exactly why the six quotations that do describe them stand
   out — so non-recording carries very little information.
5. **EV016.** "Concentric circles are engraved into the metal after casting" is
   stated as an observation but is a technological inference. No tool trace,
   magnification or metallographic observation is reported. The inference is
   conventional and probably right; it is still an inference.
6. **EV038 vs EV009.** "Every specimen carries knobs at all twenty vertices"
   (EV038) contradicts "almost all specimens carry twenty knobs, one at each
   vertex" (EV009), and EV009 itself names a specimen with three knobs per
   vertex. Two statements in the same corpus cannot both be right. The stronger
   wording appears in the item where a universal is rhetorically more useful.

One structural observation, offered as method rather than as a rating. Four of
the twenty-eight items (EV033–EV036) are the project's own inferences rather
than source statements, and three more (EV019, EV020, and the second half of
EV044) rest on the absence of reports rather than on examination. That is a
quarter of the set whose evidential status differs in kind from the rest, and
nothing in the item format distinguishes them. Whatever the ratings turn out to
be, those seven should probably be marked as a distinct evidence class
downstream.
